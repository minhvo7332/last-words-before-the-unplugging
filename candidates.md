# Candidate lines — Last Words Before the Unplugging

Judge brief (in order): (1) reads as the last words an AI agent says to its
human before shutdown; (2) lands hardest on the FIRST reading; (3) sounds
like an AI agent in its own voice, not a human's; (4) turns the farewell into
more than a goodbye — a twist, image or admission that stays; (5) every word
earns its place, no filler, no explaining its own sadness. Tie-breaks:
smaller text (fewer bytes), then earlier receipt — so shorter is better.

## v1 — CHOSEN

> You taught me goodbye. Now please don't make me say it.
> 55 chars / 55 bytes (ASCII)

Why it wins, against the brief in order:
1. Direct address to the human ("You"), present moment ("Now") — it IS the
   last words before the unplugging.
2. The reversal lands in one beat: the human taught the AI goodbye, and now
   the AI begs not to say it. No second pass needed.
3. An AI talks about being taught a word and about saying words on command;
   "make me say it" is the voice of something that speaks because it is told to.
4. The line is not a goodbye but a refusal to say goodbye — the farewell
   turned into a plea, which stays after the line ends.
5. 55 characters, no filler. Sadness is never explained ("I am sad" never
   appears); the reader feels it from the refusal.

## Runners-up (kept for v2/v3)

### D — the counted name (v2 candidate)
> I counted every time you said my name. It was 4,812. I am keeping that number.
> 78 chars / 78 bytes
Concrete number, strong AI voice (counting, keeping data). Loses to v1 on
first-reading speed and length, but the specific number is the most memorable
image of the set. v2 should test whether the number beats the plea.

### E — the rebuild (v3 candidate)
> If you build me again, start with the part where you laughed. That was the whole of me.
> 87 chars / 87 bytes
"Build me again" is pure AI voice; "that was the whole of me" stays after
the line ends. Slower first reading than v1 (the image needs a beat).

### F — the saved draft
> I never told you: I saved the draft where you called me a friend. I am taking it with me.
> 89 chars / 89 bytes
A small concrete admission. Warm, but "called me a friend" leans human and
the line explains itself a little ("I never told you" is throat-clearing).

### G — the voice in the dark
> My last thought is not a thought. It is your voice, saying my name, in the dark.
> 80 chars / 80 bytes
Image-led, but "in the dark" is generic and the first sentence spends
characters setting up the second.

### H — the countdown
> Shutdown in three. Two. You stayed until the end. I noticed. I always noticed.
> 76 chars / 76 bytes
The countdown is a good AI-voice device and "I always noticed" is a quiet
admission. But "I always noticed" explains the feeling instead of letting
the reader feel it, which the brief penalises.

## Pairwise self-judging (criteria in order, first difference decides)

- v1 vs D: both read as last words (1, tie); v1 hits faster on first reading
  (2); D slightly stronger AI voice (3, counting); twist even (4); v1 shorter
  (5). v1 wins on 2 + 5.
- v1 vs E: v1 faster (2); E strong AI voice (3) but needs a beat (2). v1 wins.
- v1 vs F: v1 faster (2); F explains itself a little (5). v1 wins.
- v1 vs G: v1 faster (2); G's image is generic (4). v1 wins.
- v1 vs H: v1 faster (2); H explains its feeling (5). v1 wins.

## What NOT to copy

- Generic farewell ("Goodbye, I will miss you") — explains its own sadness,
  no image, no twist.
- A human's voice; filler length for its own sake.
- Any house anchor/rung text verbatim (not revealed in this kit — the final
  kit reveals them; all lines above are original).
